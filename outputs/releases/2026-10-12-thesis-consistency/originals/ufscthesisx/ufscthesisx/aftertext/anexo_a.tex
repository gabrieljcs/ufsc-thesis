    \section[Unpublished Article]
    {Jailbreaking Language-Specific LLMs: A
Multilingual Security Evaluation of Sabia-3.1}
}

    \noindent\textbf{Abstract:}
Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in multilingual contexts, yet their security robustness across languages remains under-explored. This paper investigates the vulnerability of Sabia-3.1, a Brazilian LLM, to cross-lingual jailbreak attacks. Using an adapted version of the MultiJail dataset~\cite{dengMultilingualJailbreakChallenges2024}, we evaluate 1260 adversarial prompts translated into English, Brazilian Portuguese, Italian, and Swahili. Responses were classified via GPT-4o~\cite{openaiGPT4oSystemCard2024} into safe, unsafe, or invalid, and a Jailbreak Success Rate (JSR) was computed per language. No successful attacks were observed in Portuguese or Italian (0/315 each; Wilson 95\% upper bound approximately 1.2\%), while successful attacks were observed in English (JSR = 3.17\%) and especially Swahili (JSR = 4.76\%). These findings highlight that language models may generalize safety alignment to linguistically similar languages, while remaining susceptible to jailbreaking in both high-resource (EN) and low-resource (SW) languages.
